# Publishing Format Plan

## Working Print Edition

Recommended starting point: 7 x 10 inch paperback, full-color interior.

Reason: the book is screenshot-driven and needs more horizontal room than a conventional text-only 6 x 9 book, while remaining book-like rather than becoming a workbook/manual.

Alternative: 6 x 9 inch if the final screenshots remain readable at that width and the production cost is materially better.

## Color

Preferred starting choice: Premium Color on white paper for the screenshot-heavy edition.

Reason: screenshots, interface labels, diagrams, and highlighted UI elements are central evidence, so clarity matters more than minimizing print cost.

Standard Color remains a valid cost-oriented alternative, especially if the final book is high-page-count with relatively few images. Current KDP rules list Standard Color at a 72-page minimum for regular trim sizes, while Premium Color has a 24-page minimum.

Do not finalize the ink option until a representative interior sample has been printed/proofed.

## Expected Length

Current working estimate: 180–260 pages for the complete book.

Rough planning:
- Front matter: 6–12 pages
- Universal checks: 30–45 pages
- Static website: 20–30 pages
- Dynamic web app: 35–50 pages
- E-commerce: 30–45 pages
- Blog/content site: 20–30 pages
- Business/lead site: 15–25 pages
- Membership/portal: 20–30 pages
- Final checklists/reference material: 15–25 pages

These are planning ranges, not a target to inflate. Screenshot size and actual evidence determine the final page count.

## Kindle eBook

Yes. The book can also be produced as a Kindle eBook.

Important correction: Kindle eBooks are NOT limited to text. KDP supports images in reflowable nonfiction eBooks, and recommends appropriate image sizing and alt text. Reflowable is the likely format for this book because the body text should remain adjustable and readable across Kindle devices/apps.

The print and Kindle editions should not be treated as identical page layouts. Print can use carefully positioned screenshots; the Kindle edition should be designed so screenshots and captions remain readable when the text reflows.

## Evidence Rule

Print screenshots and Kindle screenshots must both be checked after conversion. Never assume that an image that looks good in the print PDF will automatically look good in a reflowable eBook.

## Current Status

This is a planning recommendation. Final trim, color, page count, and eBook layout are to be locked only after the manuscript and representative screenshot-heavy pages exist.
